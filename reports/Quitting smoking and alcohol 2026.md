# Patches won't hook you; beer might

Your plan already follows most of the 2024–2026 evidence. What needs changing is mostly about people, places and timing, not which medicine you use. Combination nicotine replacement therapy (NRT: a patch plus gum or lozenge) is a top-tier quit aid. About **12 in 100 users are still off cigarettes at six months, against about 6 in 100 with no medicine**, and head-to-head trials show **no clear difference from varenicline** ([Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/e-cigarettes-varenicline-and-cytisine-are-most-effective-stop-smoking-aids-analysis-over-150000); [Livingstone-Banks 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37142273/)). On your direct question: **no, patches very rarely keep people addicted.** They release nicotine too slowly to reinforce the habit, and only about 2 in 100 patch users are still using them months later ([West 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10823399/)). Cutting beer at the same time helps rather than overloads you. On drinking days the risk of a smoking slip is about **five times higher**, and those slips cluster in bars, among friends and around other smokers, which is the 19th hole ([Kahler 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893295)). These are the changes that matter most:

- check for alcohol dependence before Monday;
- add structured quit support, which is the plan's biggest gap;
- keep the golf course alcohol-free until at least Day 90;
- start the patch this week;
- don't diet for the first three months;
- turn the savings account into a deposit contract with a referee.

**None of the research checked what is registered, stocked or affordable in South Africa.** Confirm every product named here with your Cape Town GP or pharmacist.

## No: patches rarely hook anyone, cigarettes do

How addictive a nicotine product is depends on how fast it delivers nicotine, and the patch is the slowest product available. One trial compared the patch, gum, inhaler and nasal spray. At week 15, **2% of patch users** were still using it, against 7% for gum and inhaler and 10% for the spray. No patch user rated themselves dependent ([West 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10823399/)). US pharmacy data agree: **fewer than 1 in 100 new patch buyers** kept buying every month for six months or more ([Shiffman 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12958394/)). Vaping is very different. A year into a major trial, **9% of people who quit with NRT were still using it, against 80% of those who quit with e-cigarettes who were still vaping** ([Hajek 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30699054/)).

Even long-term use would be far safer than smoking. NICE says most smoking-related disease comes from other parts of tobacco smoke, not from nicotine. It also says licensed products can be used "for as long as they help" prevent a return to smoking ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng209/chapter/Treating-tobacco-dependence)). A trial of a full year of patch use found no safety concerns ([Schnoll 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25705872/)).

Two caveats are worth acting on. First, the gum or lozenge lingers more than the patch (about 7% against 2%), so phase it out on purpose and don't let it become a treat. Second, the bigger danger is stopping too early, not staying on too long. Extending the patch from 8 to 24 weeks raised abstinence at week 24 from **20.3% to 31.6%** ([Schnoll 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20124230/)). Going beyond 24 weeks added nothing ([Schnoll 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25705872/)). Stepping down gradually rather than stopping in one go adds about one quitter per 100, on low-certainty evidence ([Lindson 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37696529/)). Your step-down is fine but not essential.

## Twelve adjustments, led by a safety check and the 19th hole

Most of the plan should stay as written.

- **Quitting in one go.** Stopping completely on one day beats cutting down first: **22.0% against 15.5%** were abstinent at six months, even among people who preferred to cut down ([Lindson-Hawley 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26975007/)).
- **Combination NRT.** It needs no upgrade to varenicline or cytisine, because the direct comparison is a draw.
- **Alcohol.** Two weeks of zero alcohol and a permanent end to beer remove the main relapse trigger.
- **Exercise.** The 16:00 gym slot and the after-dinner walk sit at well-chosen times.

## Combination NRT already sits in the top tier of quit aids

The largest analysis yet is a 2023 Cochrane review of 319 trials and 157,000 people ([Lindson 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37696529/)). It ranks the options by how much they raise the odds of quitting (an odds ratio of 2 means double the odds of no medicine):

| Rank | Option | Odds ratio | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top tier | Nicotine e-cigarettes | 2.37 | High-certainty evidence |
| Top tier | Varenicline | 2.33 | High-certainty evidence |
| Top tier | Cytisine | 2.21 | High-certainty evidence |
| Close behind | Combination NRT | 1.93 | Its uncertainty range overlaps the top tier |
| Clearly weaker | Bupropion | 1.43 | |
| Clearly weaker | One form of NRT alone | About 1.4 | |

In plain numbers, about 6 in 100 people stay quit at six months with no medicine, 9 in 100 with one form of NRT, 12 in 100 with two forms and 14 in 100 with varenicline ([Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD015226.pub2/full)). These rates look low because they count strict, verified abstinence from a single attempt. They argue for stacking every advantage, not for despair.

Trials that compared varenicline directly with combination NRT found **no clear difference (RR 1.02)** ([Livingstone-Banks 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37142273/)). A real-world trial of 1,086 people found combination NRT, varenicline and the patch alone statistically level at both six and twelve months ([Baker 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26813210/)). NICE's February 2025 update lists combination NRT among the options "more likely" to work ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng209/chapter/Treating-tobacco-dependence)).

The right dose matters more than the brand.

- **Patch strength.** Patches stronger than 21–22 mg add nothing (RR 1.09) ([Lindson 2019](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013308/full)).
- **Gum strength.** 4 mg gum beats 2 mg (RR 1.43), mainly in heavily dependent smokers ([Lindson 2019](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013308/full)).
- **Timing.** Gum and lozenges release nicotine more slowly than a cigarette. NICE advises using them **before** the times you would usually smoke, not waiting for a craving ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng209/chapter/Treating-tobacco-dependence)).

Your plan doesn't state how many cigarettes you smoke a day. Tell the pharmacist, so the strengths can be matched to that number.

The alternatives are backups, not upgrades.

- **Varenicline** is the strongest single prescription option. The EAGLES trial of 8,144 people found no significant excess of serious psychiatric side effects compared with the patch or a placebo. About a quarter of users get nausea ([Anthenelli 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27116918/)). It is started one to two weeks before quit day ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng209/chapter/Treating-tobacco-dependence)), so switching now would delay Monday for no clear gain. Its real value for a relapser is later: longer varenicline courses reduce relapse (RR 1.23) ([Livingstone-Banks 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31684681/)).
- **Cytisine** works about as well as varenicline. It causes less nausea (10.3% against 25.1%) and is cheap ([Polosa & Cohen 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12927057/)).
- **Nicotine e-cigarettes** beat NRT in the November 2025 Cochrane update (RR 1.55, high certainty, about 3 extra quitters per 100). Long-term harms are unknown ([Cochrane 2025](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub10/full)). Most successful vapers keep vaping, so for your nicotine-free goal they are a fallback if NRT fails. Smoking and vaping together delivers none of the benefit ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng209/chapter/Treating-tobacco-dependence)).
- **Nicotine pouches** have no evidence of helping anyone quit. The first Cochrane review, in October 2025, found only four tiny studies and no demonstrable benefit ([Cochrane 2025](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD016220.pub2/full)).

## Alcohol is the lever that protects the cigarette quit

Drinking is the strongest single trigger for a smoking lapse, and the pull runs mostly one way: drink leads to cigarettes far more than cigarettes lead to drink. In heavy-drinking smokers, a drinking day carried about **five times** the lapse risk of a dry day. A heavy-drinking day carried more than eight times the risk ([Kahler 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893295)). In a lab test, a strong drink made smokers light up six minutes sooner ([Kahler 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-014-3613-3)). A 2023 systematic review found drinking linked to lower quit success in 20 of 27 studies. It advises smokers who drink to "stop or substantially reduce" alcohol during a quit attempt ([van Amsterdam 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.alcohol.2022.12.003)).

Trials back this up. Adding alcohol help to stop-smoking treatment raised quitting (adjusted OR 1.56), with the gain concentrated in the first weeks ([Kahler 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18837602/)). In a quitline trial it raised seven-month abstinence from 10.3% to 13.5% ([Toll 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25419583/)). The fear that tackling both at once backfires has no support: quitting smoking does not push drinking up ([Kahler 2010, ITC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20227840/)), and for most quitters drinking falls ([Berg 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25997014/)).

Two weeks off alcohol is a sensible reset. The best-known study is of a month off ([Mehta 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5942469)):

| Measure | Change after one month off alcohol |
|---|---|
| Blood pressure | 136/89 → 125/82 |
| Insulin resistance | Down 26% |
| Liver enzyme GGT | Down 29% |
| Weight | Down 1.5% |

People in that study chose to stop rather than being randomly assigned. Two weeks is half that time, so expect smaller changes in the same direction. A break does not cause a rebound: Dry January participants were drinking less six months later ([de Visser 2016](https://ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/view/1141)). The wine-only phase is also a legitimate goal. In randomised trials, controlled-drinking goals did about as well as abstinence goals ([Henssler 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33188563/)).

Here is how a glass on agreed evenings compares with national limits.

- **UK:** within the limit of 14 units a week ([Drinkaware](https://www.drinkaware.co.uk/low-risk-drinking-guidelines)).
- **Australia:** within the limit of 10 standard drinks a week ([Health.gov.au](https://health.gov.au/news/australian-alcohol-guidelines-revised)).
- **Canada:** at or above the low-risk band of two drinks a week ([CCSA](https://www.ccsa.ca/sites/default/files/2024-05/Canadas-Guidance-on-Alcohol-and-Health-poster-2023-en.pdf)).
- **WHO:** no level of drinking is safe ([WHO](https://www.who.int/azerbaijan/news/item/04-01-2023-no-level-of-alcohol-consumption-is-safe-for-our-health)).
- **US:** the January 2026 guidelines dropped number limits for "consume less alcohol" ([GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/01/07/trump-administration-ditches-advice-to-limit-alcohol-to-1-2-drinks-per-day/)). That followed policy decisions, not new evidence. The US Surgeon General's 2025 advisory had just linked alcohol to at least seven cancers ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/oash-alcohol-cancer-risk.pdf)).

Agree the glass size in advance; "one glass" means little unless the pour is fixed.

Alcohol-free beer is a mixed bet for you. Household purchase data suggest it mostly replaces stronger beer rather than leading back to it ([Jané Llopis 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9270994)). Its use is rising fast among people cutting down ([Buss 2025](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10211336/1/Brown_e002775.full.pdf)). But in heavy drinkers it raises craving, more so the more dependent they are ([Caballeria 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9571501/)). Half a pint of low-alcohol beer increased craving in men in one study ([Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/18472)). Your beer and your cigarettes share the same glass, bar and clubhouse ritual. So choose non-beer drinks for at least the first month. After that, try alcohol-free beer only if it doesn't stir a craving for either. No study has tested whether it triggers smoking.

Medicines for drinking are second-line for your pattern. They become relevant only if the wine-only plan fails.

- **Naltrexone and acamprosate** prevent a return to drinking in roughly one of every 11 to 18 people treated ([McPheeters 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37934220/)).
- **Nalmefene** is taken only on days you expect to drink, and cuts about three heavy-drinking days a month ([NICE TA325](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ta325/resources/nalmefene-for-reducing-alcohol-consumption-in-people-with-alcohol-dependence-82602488589253)).
- All were tested mainly in people with moderate-to-severe alcohol problems.

The genuinely new medicine story is GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide (sold as Ozempic or Wegovy).

- **Alcohol:** in the 26-week SEMALCO trial, semaglutide cut heavy-drinking days by **13.7 percentage points more than placebo** in people with alcohol use disorder and obesity ([Klausen 2026](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2826%2900305-3/fulltext)).
- **Smoking:** semaglutide reduced craving and weight but not cigarettes smoked ([Hendershot 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.14898)). In a trial of a similar drug, dulaglutide, the weight benefit had gone by one year ([Lüthi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102429)).

A GLP-1 is a reasonable later conversation with your GP, but only if you meet weight-loss prescribing criteria. Using it for alcohol or smoking is off-label (outside its approved uses).

## Cravings pass in minutes, slips are data, habits take months

The danger window is short and front-loaded.

- **Withdrawal symptoms** (irritability, anxiety, low mood, restlessness, poor sleep) peak in the first week and last two to four weeks ([Hughes 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/14622200701188919)).
- **Relapse:** in unaided attempts, most relapse happens within eight days ([Hughes 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2004.00540.x)).
- **Week two matters most.** Among patch users who smoked at all in week two, 83–97% were smoking at six months ([Kenford 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8301790/)). That is why UK specialists teach a strict "not a puff" rule for the early weeks, although the rule itself has never been tested against alternatives ([NCSCT](https://www.ncsct.co.uk/index.php/library/view/pdf/not-a-puff-rule.pdf)).
- **Cravings are shorter than they feel.** Smokers in a craving overestimate how long and how strongly an urge will last ([Sayette 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-164x.19.1.88)).

A slip is information, not a verdict. In studies that tracked smokers' first slips as they happened, how people felt about that first slip did not predict relapse. What drove the next slip was **feeling like giving up**. Doing something restorative straight away cut the chance of another slip that day ([Shiffman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-006x.64.5.993)). Self-compassion means treating a slip the way you would treat a friend's. It is consistently linked with healthier behaviour and much lower stress, though these studies show association rather than cause ([Liu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2026.2701279)). Your past relapse is also less ominous than it feels. Previous attempts predict trying again, not failing ([Vangeli 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21752135/)). Across all smokers, estimates run to about 30 attempts before one sticks, most of them made without help ([Chaiton 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27288378/)).

New habits take months, not weeks. A 2024 review puts the typical (median) time for a new habit to feel automatic at **59–66 days**. The average is 106–154 days, and individuals ranged from 4 to 335 days ([Singh 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)). Gym habits typically take months ([Buyalskaya 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2216115120)). The researcher behind the famous "66 days" says it "is not a general rule" ([Lally 2026](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally)). Counting from 26 October, expect the gym to start feeling automatic between late December and late March, and judge it then.

A fixed time and place is the cue that builds a habit, and your 16:00 slot provides one. If 16:00 is when post-round drinks usually start, it also takes you out of the riskiest setting at the riskiest hour. The same review found morning routines formed slightly stronger habits, but blocking that trigger is worth more.

Write if-then plans for the moments your job creates. For example: "If a member offers me a beer, I order sparkling water." Or: "If colleagues step out to smoke, I take a lozenge and walk the other way." These plans raise quit rates for smoking ([McWilliams 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/hea0000768)), but they barely dent binge drinking ([Cooke 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/dar.13553)). For beer, avoiding the setting matters more than planning inside it. Treat a missed gym day like a slip: next session, no penalty.

With your wife, everyday behaviour matters more than any formal programme. Trials designed to boost partner support did not improve quitting (RR 0.97), mostly because they failed to change what partners actually did ([Faseru 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002928.pub4)). Planning the quit together beat planning alone by only 33% to 30% ([Buitenhuis 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2019.1703983)). Your "don't believe me, just watch" framing fits the evidence: watching is how her confidence rebuilds, and her confidence measurably helps. If she smokes, quitting together is the strongest move available. In couples over 50, men whose partner also quit succeeded 48% of the time, against 8% when she kept smoking ([Jackson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.7554)).

## Exercise cuts cravings fast; weight gain is real but outweighed

A single bout of moderate exercise is one of the most reliable craving tools known. Pooled data from 19 trials show moderate activity cut craving by about 35 points on a 100-point scale, against about 9 for light activity ([Haasova 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-014-3450-4)). The relief lasts roughly 30–50 minutes ([Taylor 2007](https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/the-acute-effects-of-exercise-on-cigarette-cravings-withdrawal-sy-2/); [Singh 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2026.101138)). A 20-minute walk also cut alcohol craving in heavy social drinkers while they walked ([Gavigan 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2026.108684)). That puts your after-dinner walk on a classic smoking trigger; keep it brisk.

For staying quit long-term, the evidence is weaker. Cochrane found no proven benefit (RR 1.08, low certainty) ([Ussher 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002295.pub6)), while a 2026 analysis of 59 trials found a modest one (RR 1.15) ([Singh 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2026.101138)). Treat the gym as craving first aid and weight control, not as the thing that keeps you off cigarettes.

Expect some weight gain and plan around it. People who quit without help gain on average **1.1 kg in the first month, 2.9 kg by three months and 4.7 kg by a year**. The spread is wide: 16% lose weight and 13% gain more than 10 kg ([Aubin 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22782848/)). Dropping beer pulls the other way. A pint of 4% lager carries about 180 kcal ([Drinkaware](http://www.drinkaware.co.uk/alcohol-facts/alcoholic-drinks-units/lager)), so a five-pint golf day is roughly 900 kcal before the bar snacks. NRT slightly delays gain, by about 0.5 kg at the end of treatment. Exercise programmes left quitters about 2 kg lighter at a year, on low-certainty evidence. No weight-control method has solid evidence of a lasting effect ([Hartmann-Boyce 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34611902/)). Weights add muscle, so track your waist as well as the scale.

Keep the stakes in proportion. In a 25-year study, recent quitters had about half the heart-disease and stroke risk of continuing smokers, and their weight gain did not change that ([Clair 2013](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.1644)). Within one to two years, heart-attack risk drops sharply ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/benefits-of-quitting.html)). Quitters also report less anxiety, depression and stress than people who keep smoking, which undercuts the idea that cigarettes relieve stress ([Taylor 2014](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.g1151)).

## Conclusion

The evidence changes where this attempt will be won or lost. At the top tier, the choice of medicine barely matters. The outcome turns on three predictable moments that no prescription covers: the first beer offered at work in week one, the first slip, and the first time the scale goes up in month two. Each has a planned answer above. Two of them happen inside your job, which makes your working hours, not your medicine cabinet, the main battleground. The 16:00 gym slot is already the most strategically placed part of your plan.

The timelines also run in opposite directions. Smoking risk is concentrated in the first eight days and the first month. The payoffs from the gym, the weight plan and your wife's renewed trust arrive in months three to six. That argues for a sequence rather than one big launch:

1. Protect the quit hard until Day 28 (Sun 22 Nov).
2. Keep the clubhouse dry until NRT ends in late January.
3. Then turn deliberately to weight loss.

For the next 90 days, every decision about alcohol is first a decision about cigarettes.
