# Playbook

The evidence behind this is in [the research report](reports/Quitting%20smoking%20and%20alcohol%202026.md).

## Smoking

**The rule: not a single puff.**

**Your quit kit: patches plus gum or lozenge, and support.** Combination nicotine replacement is a top-tier quit aid. About 12 in 100 users are off cigarettes at 6 months, against about 6 in 100 with no medicine, and it performs about the same as varenicline in head-to-head trials. Adding a counsellor, quitline or app on top makes it work better still.

- **Patch:** a steady base. Started Fri 23 Oct, a few days before quit day, which raises the odds of staying quit. Patches stronger than 21–22 mg add nothing.
- **Gum or lozenge:** 4 mg if you smoke a lot, 2 mg otherwise. Use it **before** the times you'd normally smoke, not only when a craving hits.
- **Support:** the National Council Against Smoking Quitline, **011 720 3145** ([againstsmoking.co.za](https://www.againstsmoking.co.za/)), or the GoSmokeFree programme at a Clicks clinic (Clicks Blue Route has one). Aim for 3–5 contacts over the first month. Add an acceptance-based (ACT) quit app such as iCanQuit, if it's available here.
- **Backups:** varenicline or cytisine, if the GP says they're available in South Africa. Their main value for you is later, as relapse insurance.
- **Avoid:** vapes and nicotine pouches. Most people who quit with a vape are still vaping a year later, and pouches have no evidence of helping anyone quit.

**Patches won't keep you hooked.** A patch releases nicotine slowly, without the hit a cigarette gives you in seconds. About 2 in 100 patch users are still using them months later. The smoke is what does the damage, not the nicotine. The bigger risk is stopping too early: 24 weeks on the patch beat 8 weeks.

- Highest strength until cravings are rare: at least 6–8 weeks.
- Then step down over 2–4 weeks. Drop the gum or lozenge first; it's the part that can linger.
- Aim to be off by Day 90 (Sat 23 Jan 2027). If cravings or near-misses are still common, keep going to about 24 weeks (mid-April). That is not failure.

**When a craving hits** (it passes in minutes, and always feels longer):

1. **Delay.** Tell yourself five minutes.
2. **Breathe.** Ten slow, deep breaths.
3. **Drink** a glass of water.
4. **Move.** A brisk 10-minute walk is the strongest craving killer known; the relief lasts 30–50 minutes.

**If-then plans.** Write these on paper on Sunday night and keep the card in your wallet. Plans like these doubled quit rates in one trial (10.7% against 4.9%). Edit them to fit your real days.

| If… | Then I… |
|---|---|
| a member or client offers me a beer | order sparkling water with lime |
| the guys step out to smoke | take a lozenge and walk the other way |
| I have a bad hole or a bad round | do 10 breaths and finish the hole with water |
| I finish my coffee | have gum ready and change where I drink it for 2 weeks |
| I finish a meal | get up from the table and brush my teeth or walk |
| cashflow stress hits | walk around the block before I do anything else |
| I'm driving | gum in the car; no cigarettes in the car, ever |
| it's 16:00 and the bar is calling | go to the gym; it's in the calendar |

**The slip drill.** One cigarette is a slip, not a relapse. What predicts the next one is feeling like giving up, so act straight away:

1. Keep the patch on. It slows the slide from a slip to a relapse.
2. Bin the rest. Don't buy a pack.
3. Walk for 10 minutes.
4. Tell her yourself, the same day.
5. Write the trigger in the slip log in [LOG.md](LOG.md), and add an if-then plan for it.
6. Carry on that same day. No "starting again on Monday".

**Weight:** most people put on a few kilos after quitting, about 3 kg by three months on average. It's expected. Dropping beer and training offset it, and the heart benefit of quitting holds even with the weight gain.

## Alcohol

- **Until Day 28 (Sun 22 Nov):** no alcohol at all. On drinking days the risk of a smoking slip is about five times higher.
- **From Mon 23 Nov:** beer stays off. One glass of wine with her, at home, on agreed evenings. Agree the pour size first. Never on golf days, not alone, not "just one more".
- **The golf course and the club stay alcohol-free** until at least Day 90 and in practice for good. Alcohol-linked slips happen mostly in bars, with friends, around other smokers.
- **Golf, camp, social:** soda water and lime, or a soft drink. Skip alcohol-free beer for at least the first month: it can stir cravings in heavy drinkers, and your beer and cigarettes share the same ritual. After that, try it only if it doesn't make you want either.
- ⚠️ **Safety:** if you've been drinking heavily every day and get shakes, sweats, a racing heart or bad anxiety after stopping, see a doctor straight away. Stopping suddenly after heavy daily drinking can be dangerous.
- **If the wine-only plan stops working:** ask the GP about medicines for drinking (naltrexone, acamprosate). They're a second line, not a first.

## Training

The goal is to get stronger, hold your weight while you quit, and feel better. Your golf will improve too. Exercise is craving first aid; it won't keep you off cigarettes on its own.

Gym every day, 16:00–17:00. That slot replaces the late-afternoon beer, which makes it the best-placed part of the whole plan.

- **Mon / Wed / Fri:** weights, alternating workouts A and B.
- **Tue / Thu / Sat:** cardio and core (see below).
- **Sun:** easy recovery: a swim, an easy bike ride, stretching, or 9 holes walking. Rest if your body is wrecked.
- **Every evening:** a brisk 30-minute walk after dinner, which is the old cigarette moment. On golf days, walk the course.
- **Warm-up:** 5 minutes on a bike or rower, then a couple of light sets of the first exercise.
- **Weeks 1–2:** 2 sets of each exercise. **Week 3 onwards:** 3 sets.
- **Choosing a weight:** end each set feeling you could do 2–3 more reps. Once you hit the top of the rep range on every set, go up a little next time.
- A few sessions with a trainer to learn form is money well spent.
- **Habits take months, not weeks.** Expect the gym to feel automatic somewhere between late December and late March. A missed day is like a slip: next session, no penalty.

**Workout A**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Goblet squat | 3 × 8–10 |
| Dumbbell bench press (or push-ups) | 3 × 8–10 |
| Seated cable row | 3 × 10–12 |
| Plank | 3 × 30 s |
| Farmer's carry | 3 × 30 m |

**Workout B**

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift (dumbbells) | 3 × 8–10 |
| Lat pulldown | 3 × 10–12 |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 3 × 8–10 |
| Reverse lunge | 2 × 8 each leg |
| Side plank | 2 × 20 s each side |

**Rotation:** week 1 is A / B / A, week 2 is B / A / B, and so on.

**Cardio and core (Tue / Thu / Sat)**

- Warm-up: 5 minutes easy.
- Cardio, 25–30 minutes: incline treadmill walk, bike or rower. Weeks 1–2: at a pace where you can still talk. Week 3 onwards: add intervals, 6 × (1 minute hard, 2 minutes easy).
- Core and mobility, 10–15 minutes: dead bug, bird dog, side plank, hip and upper-back rotations (good for your golf swing).

## Food

**Until Day 90: hold your weight, don't diet.** Dieting while quitting was linked to lower quit rates. Dropping beer, the gym and the walks will do the work. A pint of lager is about 180 kcal, so a five-pint golf day was roughly 900 kcal before the bar snacks.

- Protein at every meal: a palm-sized portion of eggs, meat, fish, yoghurt or beans.
- A big glass of water before each meal.
- For snack cravings: fruit, a small handful of nuts, sugar-free gum.
- Golf days: eat before you go. The burrito isn't the problem; burrito, beer and smokes as the default is.
- Weigh in and measure your waist on Monday mornings under the same conditions, and write them in [LOG.md](LOG.md). The weights add muscle, so the waist tells you more than the scale.
- Personalised help from a dietitian is fine at any point. Generic diet rules are what to avoid for now.

**From Mon 1 Feb 2027: the fat-loss phase.**

- Half the plate vegetables at dinner.
- The kitchen closes after dinner.
- A modest calorie cut, with protein kept high to protect the muscle you've built.

## Skin

Golf means years of sun. Daily sunscreen is the one step with hard evidence: people who used it every day showed no detectable skin ageing over 4.5 years. Be sceptical of claims that quitting "reverses" skin ageing; it helps, but the evidence is thin.

- **Morning (1 min):** cleanser (or just water), moisturiser, broad-spectrum SPF 30–50 on face, neck, ears and the backs of your hands.
- **Night (1 min):** cleanser, moisturiser.
- **On the course:** an SPF stick in the golf bag, reapplied at the turn, plus a hat.

Keep the products next to your toothbrush so the routine rides on a habit you already have.

## Family

She'll believe day 30, not day 1.

- **Show, don't promise.** No big speeches. Do the thing, every day.
- **Agree what "watching" means.** She notices and says what she sees; she doesn't check, quiz or remind. Daily pressure from the people closest to you backfires; her confidence in you helps.
- **You report, she doesn't police.** If you slip, tell her yourself, the same day.
- **Warn her about week 1.** Nicotine withdrawal makes you irritable. Tell her it's coming and that it isn't about her.
- **Be present.** Phone away at dinner.
- **One thing together each week:** the after-dinner walk, a dinner out, the glass of wine from Day 29.
- **Don't ask for credit.** If she notices, great. If she doesn't yet, keep going.

## Money

Putting health first doesn't cost wealth; it starts paying straight away.

- Work out your weekly spend on cigarettes and beer, plus food and drinks at the course. Write it in [LOG.md](LOG.md).
- Every week, move that amount into a separate account. It pays for the gym and your skincare, and the rest is for the two of you.

**The deposit contract.** Money on the line raises quit rates by about half, and the effect lasts after the deal ends. Set it up this weekend:

1. **The stake:** put up front a sum you'd hate to lose. The size matters less than the sting.
2. **The referee:** one of the guys from camp. Not your wife: she stays a supporter, not an inspector.
3. **The checks:** a carbon-monoxide breath test on Day 14, Day 30 and Day 90, at a pharmacy, the GP or a stop-smoking service, or on a home CO monitor. A nicotine (cotinine) strip won't work while you're on patches; it will read positive anyway.
4. **The rule:** fail a check or smoke, and the stake goes to a charity the referee picks. Pass, and you get it back.

Write the details in [LOG.md](LOG.md). That's cashflow you didn't have before.
